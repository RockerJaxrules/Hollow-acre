# Part 2: "The Deep Workings": the full story (outline v2: approved by the owner, "Yes, build on it")

The owner (2026-11-19): "story needs to have way more deph realism and needs to be until dawn/quarry length". This is the
long version: about 8-10 hours, ten chapters with a prologue and an ending, a cast whose lives the player's choices keep
or lose, cutscenes in first person, and the creature hunting throughout. It keeps everything already set down (the
letter from R. Kessler, the crash, grandmother's truck and journal, Camp Nine, the husks as the changed miners, Coldwater,
Caitie-Lynn, the long climb to the company town at the top). It is our own: no story, character, place or mechanic is
taken from Until Dawn or The Quarry; what is learned from them is craft (pacing, dread, choices that cost lives).

## What it is about
Grief and inheritance. The player came up the mountain because a grandmother they barely knew left them a company and
then vanished. The mountain tells them who she was: the woman who reopened the deep workings for money, watched the men
who worked them change, lied to the families, and finally went up alone to undo it. Every chapter asks the same thing in
a new place: what do you owe the people who are left?

## Realism (the rules the world keeps)
- No magic explanation until late, and even then a physical one: the seam is a vein of something cold and alive in the
  rock (an ancient extremophile organism in black ice) that gets into men through the lungs on long shifts. It starves
  them, stops them feeling cold or pain, lengthens their bones, and makes them hunt warm things at night. Light hurts its
  eyes; fire hurts it; bullets slow it but rarely stop it (they are men's bodies driven past pain).
- **The creature is hard to kill.** A rifle round knocks one down or drives it off; killing one takes fire, a fall,
  dynamite, or many hits. Most nights you run, hide and survive.
- People behave like people. They are scared, practical, and they lie. Doors are barred at dusk. Nobody explains
  everything. Mining is real work: lamps, timbering, ventilation, ore carts, blasting, the company store's scrip.
- The cold is a threat on its own: body heat, wet clothes, frostbite, fires that die.

## The cast (each can live or die by the player's choices)
- **The player** (Part 1's character, made real): the grandchild who inherited the Hollow Mining Company.
- **Grandmother, Eleanor Hollow**: seen only in her journal, her tapes and photographs, until the end.
- **Ruth Kessler**, foreman, 60s: hard, short-spoken, kind. She ran the deep crews and lost her son Danny to the seam.
  (The letter's "R. Kessler"; the shot plan's "he" becomes she, or stays a man, as the owner prefers.)
- **Danny Kessler**: Ruth's son, one of the first husks. The creature that rammed the car has his face.
- **Tomas Reyes**, the Coldwater mechanic: wry, broke, owes the company. He can rebuild the car.
- **Caitie-Lynn Coyle**: runs Coldwater's bar; Caitlyn's sister; knows everything about the player from her sister's
  letters (the running gag; she is also the bravest person in town). Later, with the owner's photos.
- **Pastor Abel Lund**: keeps the church's wall of missing miners' names; knows what the company paid him to say.
- **June Achterberg**, 15: a miner's daughter who sneaks out at night looking for her father, who walked into the snow.
- **Doc Marsh**: the clinic's tired doctor, who has the only autopsy of a husk and burned the report.
- **Vic Hale**, the company lawyer sent up to "settle" with the families now that the player owns the company; he wants
  the records and the player's signature, and he is willing to leave people behind to get them.

## Systems that carry the length
- **Choices with consequences**: in cutscenes and in play (answer, act, or say nothing), each marked consequence carries
  forward; a wrong choice can get a character killed chapters later. A chapter-end "what you changed" screen in the
  game's own style (a page of grandmother's ledger, lines struck through or kept).
- **Her tapes** (her voice is never heard: a tape recorder shows its reel turning and her words appear as captions):
  20 tapes across the mountain, each a piece of what she did.
- **Tally tags**: brass miners' tags, 40 of them, each with the name of a missing man; the church wall fills in as they
  are returned. Bring them all and the ending changes.
- **Survival**: the flashlight's battery, body heat, stamina, hiding, sound (running and dropped things are heard).
- **Bases**: the shed, then the bar in Coldwater, then the dam's control room: fortified for the nights.
- **The car**: rebuilt over the game (parts found and bought); driving it up the switchbacks is the final act's opening.
- **Co-op** for up to four throughout (the others as the player's companions on the climb).

## The chapters (each about 45-60 minutes, each ending in a cutscene)
**Prologue: The Letter** (Part 1's ending; the drive; the crash). Done.

**1. The Bottom** (The Drift, Camp Nine): waking in the wreck; grandmother's truck; her journal; the shed; the first
night; the lantern and the rifle; Kessler. *Choice:* hide in the shed when the lantern comes, or go out to it (out: you
see her face before the creature's charge; in: she has to come in for you and is clawed, carrying the wound into
chapter 3).

**2. Morning at Camp Nine**: the blood trail into the trees by daylight; the camp explored (the bunkhouse, the store's
scrip ledgers, the assay office with the first sample of black ice in a jar); Kessler tells part of it. The first tape.
A husk asleep in the bunkhouse cellar by day. *Choice:* burn the bunkhouse with it inside, or leave it (left: it hunts
you on the Ore Line).

**3. The Ore Line**: the old railway up the gorge on foot: snowsheds, a trestle, a dark tunnel, an empty station, the
frozen lake. The trestle chase (a husk on the timbers behind you). June, alone in the tunnel, looking for her father.
*Choice:* take June with you or send her back down the line (sent back: she does not reach Coldwater).

**4. Coldwater**: a real town with a handful of people: the bar (Caitie-Lynn), the garage (Tomas), the church (Lund),
the clinic (Doc Marsh), the company store. Doors barred at dusk. The town meeting: the families want the company to pay
or burn. Vic Hale arrives on the last snowcat before the pass closes. The first night in town: a husk at the bar's
window, a face the town knows. *Choice:* tell the town what the journal says, or keep it quiet (told: panic, a family
flees into the night).

**5. The Company's Books**: by day, mining the old workings above town for ore to sell (the first mine level: timbering,
ore carts, lamps, gas); the company offices: payroll, the deep crews' medical logs, the cover-up in grandmother's hand.
Hale wants the books; the player can give them up, hide them, or copy them. Doc Marsh's burned report, half-saved.
*Choice:* sign Hale's settlement (money for parts; the families get nothing) or refuse.

**6. Deadwood**: the frozen dead forest, the sawmill and logging camp on the way to the dam; an avalanche field. The
husks are more and bolder. Someone from town is taken at the sawmill (who depends on earlier choices). The sawmill's
saw as a weapon, a blaze as a trap. Tapes 9-12: what grandmother saw on the deep shift's last day.

**7. Halfway**: the dam and its power station, the holdouts who keep the lights on, the weather station on the ridge
(radio, the storm coming). Getting the power back to the lift in the mine. Danny Kessler's face at the station's window:
Ruth sees her son. *Choice:* let Ruth go after him or hold her back (let go: she does not come back; held: she never
forgives you, and saves you later anyway).

**8. The Car**: back down to Coldwater with the last parts; Tomas rebuilds it in one long night under siege, the
garage the base; the player defends it. The car's first start (cutscene). Hale makes his move (he takes the snowcat and
the books, and leaves someone behind).

**9. The Switchbacks and the Pass**: the drive up the road you fell from, past the guardrail gap and the crash site from
above (the wreck small below), the snowsheds, the pass in the storm; the company town at the top: offices, headframe,
grandmother's house, all lights out.

**10. The Deep Workings**: grandmother's house (her study, the last tapes); then down the shaft in the cage to the deep
level, into the seam. What she became: still herself, barely, keeping the husks in the dark with fire and failing. The
choice of the ending: seal the mine with everyone (and her) inside, or try to bring her out.

**Endings**: who is alive, who the town forgives, whether the seam is sealed, whether the company is kept, sold or
buried, whether all 40 names are on the church wall. From "everyone lives, the mine sealed, the town paid" to "you come
down alone in the spring thaw". The last cutscene: the drive down in daylight, or the house on the hill, its one light on.

## Place size (the world)
One real-scale mountain (about 6 x 6 km, 1,500 m of climb) streamed with World Partition: The Drift, the Ore Line gorge,
Coldwater, Deadwood, Halfway, the Switchbacks, the Top, and the deep workings under it; each a hand-dressed place built
from Megascans and our own models, with abandoned places between (cabins, a sanatorium for the deep crews, a collapsed
adit, a frozen waterfall, a logging chute).
