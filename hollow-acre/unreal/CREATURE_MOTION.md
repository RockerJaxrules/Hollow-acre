# The husk's movement: what it is measured against

The owner: the creature "moves silly and doesn't feel right"; use Until Dawn and The Quarry as the reference for its
movement. Studied frame by frame from gameplay/cutscene videos (references only, nothing copied or shipped):
Until Dawn's Wendigos (a charge at the camera, the "don't move" close-up) and The Quarry's werewolves (a prowl down a
jetty, a confrontation that ends in it leaving).

## What reads as wrong in ours (the v13 capture)
- The stare: it rises from all fours to a straight-legged, upright man with the arms hanging at its sides: a mannequin.
  The director also played that stretch of the take at 12% speed, so the rise was a slow, floaty blend with no life.
- The scream: both arms thrown straight up over its head, the body upright: it reads as "hands up" or cheering.
- On all fours: the hands set wide of the shoulders and the back flat: a crab or a frog, not a predator.
- The drop to all fours before it bolts: a squat.

## The targets
1. **On all fours (The Quarry's prowl).** The back arched into a hump at the shoulders, the hips high, the hands set
   under the shoulders (not wide), the head low between the shoulders and steady, the eyes on what it watches; each
   forelimb placed deliberately.
2. **Holding still and staring.** Low and coiled, never upright: knees deeply bent, pelvis tipped forward, back
   hunched, the long arms hanging in front of the knees, the head jutting forward and low with the face on its target.
   Life comes from breathing (the chest heaving) and the fingers flexing, broken by sudden head jerks: a sharp tilt to
   one side (a few frames), held, snapped back (Until Dawn's head comes in slowly at a strong tilt and hangs there).
   Played at real speed: stillness and snaps, not slow motion.
3. **The scream (Until Dawn's charge at the camera).** An explosive thrust in a fraction of a second, then held: the
   torso pitched forward at its target, the head thrust forward and down with the jaw wide, the arms flung out wide to
   the sides at about shoulder height, elbows a little bent, claws open, palms toward its target; mid-stride, weight
   forward. A tremor through it while it screams. Never arms overhead.
4. **Going.** A pounce down onto the hands, not a squat; away at a gallop with the head low and level.

These are the checks for every pass (see the frame strips in each capture's review).
