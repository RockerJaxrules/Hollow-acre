# The crash cutscene, rebuilt (shot plan, for the owner's approval)

The owner: the first version is bad; use the horror and cinematic techniques of The Quarry and Until Dawn, but in first
person. Studied (references only, nothing copied): Supermassive's hard light and dark (Renaissance chiaroscuro: small
warm pools against cold moonlight and beams), composed shots lit shot by shot, slow quiet build-ups and false calm before
a scare, the monster glimpsed in the background before the character sees it, breathing and an unsteady camera at the
worst moments, scares played inside the cutscene, and cuts that never disorient. In first person every one of those
has to happen through the driver's own eyes: where the head turns, what the eyes focus on, blinking and blacking out
instead of cutting, and sound.

## What was wrong with the first one
Ten seconds of uneventful driving; the creature a small pale figure far off, flat in the headlights, its face never
seen; no build-up, no silence before the scare; sixteen seconds of unreadable white tumbling with specks on the glass;
a still headlight in the snow to end; the driver's bare hands on the wheel read as feet; an engine message in the
first frame.

## The new cut (about 80 seconds, letterboxed, graded cold, film grain)
1. **Black.** Wipers, the engine, wind on the body. A line of text: the mountain, November.
2. **The drive.** Fade in. Headlights are the only light; snow streaks through the beams; the dash glows warm.
   The driver glances down at the passenger seat: Kessler's letter, a photo of grandmother clipped to it (focus pulls
   to it, then back to the road). Gloved hands on the wheel (winter driving gloves).
3. **The gap.** The beams sweep over a smashed gap in the guardrail, tyre tracks going over the edge into the dark.
   The car slows; the head turns to follow it past. A low drone rises. (Someone went over here before you.)
   In the headlights: a dark blood trail in the snow running to the edge, a torn glove caught on the twisted rail.
4. **False calm.** Back to the road. The snow thickens. Breathing.
5. **Eyes in the trees.** At the edge of the beams on the left, two pale points low in the tree line catch the light,
   then blink out. The head turns to them. Nothing.
6. **On the roof.** No warning: the eyes are on the road when it drops onto the roof from above with a bang that
   jolts the car (it is never seen getting on, and never runs past first). The driver brakes hard. The eyes flinch up at
   the roof, then go back to the road and stay there, frozen: claws scraping over the metal, the roof creaking, a
   breath, a heartbeat. Nothing gives away where it will come from (the owner: never look ahead to where it will be).
   (The owner chose the roof: at 2.9 m tall, crouched on the bonnet its head stays above the windshield.)
7. **The face.** Everything drains to near silence; then all at once its face swings down into the windshield, upside
   down, to just above the driver's eye line (a string cluster, a low boom); the eyes snap to it; back into the seat.
   (Unseen above: it lies flat on the roof, legs stretched back, hands on the front edge, its upper body and neck
   curled over the edge; Unreal solves the pose each frame so its head is exactly where it should be.)
8. **The attack.** It draws its head back and slams it into the glass: the windshield cracks, a spider web bursting out
   from the blow (laminated glass: it holds, and stays cracked for the rest of the scene), flakes falling inside, the
   view jolting. It shrieks at the driver through the cracks, jaw wide, head shaking (the owner: it should be trying to
   get at you, not just there to scare you). Its bloody claw comes down and strikes the glass beside its face (a
   second crack) and drags down it.
9. **Gone.** It pulls its face off the glass and is gone up over the edge; it springs off the back of the roof and
   lands on the road behind. Silence. A ragged breath. The cracked glass.
10. **The creep.** The car rolls on slowly. The head checks the right, the rear-view mirror, the road ahead. Its steps
    run up the left side, unseen.
11. **The blow.** The head turns: its face is at the driver's window. The brake, hard. Slow motion: the window bursts
    inward, glass pebbles drifting past the eyes, its face and claws filling the window.
12. **Over.** Back to full speed: the rail splits, the headlights tip down the chute. The fall is shown in a few clear
    glimpses with blackouts between them (the driver blacking out): the slope rushing up, a tree, the sky upside down.
13. **Black.** Ringing ears, a heartbeat.
14. **Waking.** Eyes open, blurred, blinking: hanging upside down in the belt (the car on its roof, its nose propped on a
    drift, the snow above and the night below through the glass), one headlight flickering into the snow,
    snow sifting in through the broken window, the radio hissing. In the headlight, outside, a tall silhouette walks
    slowly past and is gone.
15. **Eyes close.** Black. The title.

It is one continuous creature, never created or removed where it can be seen, and it moves fast but never jumps
from place to place (the owner: no teleporting). It collides with the car: its body is traced against the car's real
surfaces every frame and pushed clear, so no part of it passes into the bonnet, the glass, the roof or the door;
gravity keeps it resting on what it is on (the roof, the road, the snow), never floating.

Gore (the owner: realistic gore throughout the game): in this cut, the gap's blood trail and glove, and the frozen
blood on the creature.

## Sound
Recorded sounds as now (all CC0, logged), placed and mixed to the shots, plus our own low drones and stings for the
build-ups (no music library). Silence is used: before the face, after it leaves, before the blow.
