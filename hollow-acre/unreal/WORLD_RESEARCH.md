# World Research: Until Dawn (2015 / 2024 UE5 remake) and The Quarry (2022)

Craft lessons for Hollow Acre Part 2 (first-person, snowy mountain mining camp, UE 5.8). Researched 2026-11-18.

**Source note.** Supermassive has published very little on environment art or lighting. The best first-hand sources are Will Byles on lighting and cameras (MCV), Adele Cutting / Soundcuts on The Quarry's audio (A Sound Effect), Barney Pratt on Until Dawn's audio (MCV, GDC 2019), Jason Graves on the score (PlayStation Blog), and Digital Domain on The Quarry's faces (80.lv). No Digital Foundry article, GDC talk or ArtStation breakdown covers the environments or lighting in detail; several pages (Unreal Engine interviews, ArtStation, fan wiki) blocked fetching. Lines marked *(inference)* are my reading of the games, not something a developer said.

---

## 1. World building and level layout

- **A small set of strong, named landmarks.** Until Dawn's mountain is built around a few landmark sets, the lodge, cable car, sanatorium, mines and radio tower, joined by trails. The mines were tin and radium workings abandoned after a 1952 collapse ([fandom: North West Mines](https://until-dawn.fandom.com/wiki/North_West_Mines)). The sanatorium was modelled as a ruin detailed enough that a later team rebuilt it in its 1950s state, "like a jigsaw puzzle" ([David Grant, ArtStation](https://david_grant.artstation.com/projects/qGRqR)). Lesson: model the history first, then wreck it, so the ruin reads as a real place that used to work.
- **Corridors pretending to be wilderness.** Both games are linear paths dressed as open forest. Reviewers note The Quarry's locations feel "siloed off from one another" ([Gamewatcher](https://www.gamewatcher.com/reviews/the-quarry-review/13339)). *(inference)* Scale comes from distant vistas (the moon, ridgelines, the camp seen from above) rather than from walkable area. For a first-person game, keep the playable path narrow and the backdrop huge.
- **Grounded in a real region.** Soundcuts researched native insects and wildlife of the Catskills-area setting so the forest sounded right ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)). Do the same for the look: pick a real mountain range and copy its tree species, rock type and snow behaviour.
- **Genre-legible places.** The Quarry's camp deliberately echoes *Friday the 13th*'s Crystal Lake and backwoods films like *Deliverance* ([SVG, Byles](https://www.svg.com/940858/director-will-byles-on-the-quarry-until-dawn-horror-film-inspirations-and-more-exclusive-interview/)). Players read the danger of a place from its archetype before anything happens.
- **Storytelling props as clue chains.** Both games use collectible clues and totems/tarot cards that build the history and foreshadow deaths ([Fangoria](https://www.fangoria.com/original/the-quarry-a-seriously-sinister-summer-camp/); [Gameland: remake changes](https://gameland.gg/heres-everything-new-and-different-in-the-until-dawn-remaster/)). Byles' rule: players should be "so warned along the way" that a death feels earned ([SVG](https://www.svg.com/940858/director-will-byles-on-the-quarry-until-dawn-horror-film-inspirations-and-more-exclusive-interview/)).
- **Interactions tied to the place.** Until Dawn began as a first-person torch game for PlayStation Move ([PlayStation LifeStyle](https://playstationlifestyle.net/?p=506353)); leftover tasks like restoring power to buildings still shape its pacing ([Giant Bomb analysis](https://www.giantbomb.com/profile/gamer_152/blog/schlock-and-awe-an-analysis-of-until-dawn/114515/)). Generators, fuse boxes and lamps make a camp feel functional.
- **Remake lesson.** Ballistic Moon added locations, interactions and collectibles and swapped fixed cameras for an over-the-shoulder view ([PushSquare](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2024/01/schlock-horror-favourite-until-dawn-being-rebuilt-for-ps5-pc)). Once the player can look anywhere, every corner needs dressing; there is no "off-camera" anymore. That applies doubly to first person.

## 2. Lighting and atmosphere

- **Chiaroscuro, not just darkness.** Byles aimed for Renaissance-style light/dark contrast "without simply making everything darker", and warned that engines fight you: "If something gets too dark, the engine will try to brighten it" ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/creating-the-atmosphere-of-until-dawn/)). In UE5 that means locking auto-exposure and controlling Lumen's skylight bounce.
- **Kill ambient fill.** Ambient light from probes and image-based lighting worked against the look, so they used few real-time lights and replaced density with luminance ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/creating-the-atmosphere-of-until-dawn/)).
- **Cold moon, warm pools, cold beams.** Outdoors: moonlight plus small warm pools around characters carrying torches. Indoors: warm candles and practicals against cold flashlight beams. Particles and volumetric lighting support both ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/creating-the-atmosphere-of-until-dawn/)).
- **Per-shot and per-character rigs.** Cutscenes and choices got per-shot lighting; exploration used rigs that lit only specific characters; hair shading let backlight show through ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/creating-the-atmosphere-of-until-dawn/)). UE5 equivalent: lighting channels and Sequencer-spawned lights per shot.
- **The remake's warning.** UE5's relight with ray-traced shadows, AO and reflections ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/until-dawn-remake-pc-ps5-unreal-engine-5-full-camera-control/)) was criticised as brighter and blander. Critics said it lost the original's blue film grade and defined moon shadows, drifting to "dusk-style lighting" ([OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/news/6766/until-dawn-remake-is-being-criticized-for-looking-way-worse-than-the-original); [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/?p=3729479); [FutureFive](https://futurefive.com.au/story/game-review-until-dawn-ps5)). Lesson: physically correct GI makes nights grey; keep a stylised, blue-biased grade and one dominant moon direction with crisp shadows.
- **Moon as a character.** The Quarry gives moon pan shots their own sound design ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)). Use the moon as a recurring compositional landmark.
- **Ship-quality tech matters.** The remake launched with shader-compile stutter during cutscenes and broken ray tracing on PC ([DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/pc-performance-analyses/until-dawn-remake-pc-performance-analysis)). Precompile PSOs and test cutscenes on a cold cache.
- **Flashlight.** Until Dawn's origin was a torch-led horror game ([PlayStation LifeStyle](https://playstationlifestyle.net/?p=506353)); its beams are cold-white against warm interiors ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/creating-the-atmosphere-of-until-dawn/)). *(inference)* The beam is the player's only fill light, so it should cast shadows, show volumetric scatter in snow and fog, and leave the frame edges black.

## 3. Cinematics and cutscenes

- **Game cameras are continuous; film cameras are edited.** Byles blended the two and set rules from testing, e.g. never make a 90-degree cut at a junction ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/creating-the-atmosphere-of-until-dawn/)). In first person, the equivalent rule is: never take the camera from the player without a motivated move (a head turn, a stumble).
- **Editorial framing.** "We can be editorial in our framing": a single character in a tighter lens beats a crowded wide ([SVG](https://www.svg.com/940858/director-will-byles-on-the-quarry-until-dawn-horror-film-inspirations-and-more-exclusive-interview/)). Tight framing hides what you can't afford and builds dread.
- **Capture first, shoot later.** The Quarry recorded full performances with no camera operator and built shots afterwards in a modified Unreal Sequencer ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/why-the-quarry-s-director-says-the-teen-horror-genre-is-great-for-games)). Author your animation as a full continuous performance, then cut cameras over it.
- **Faces.** Digital Domain scanned each actor, captured in full suits with head rigs, used ML eye tracking (modified Gaze ML) and kept micro-expressions as captured: "An animator didn't go in and fix that." Only about 27 of 4,500 shots needed hand tweaks ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/the-quarry-used-the-facial-animation-tool-made-for-thanos); [Digital Domain](https://digitaldomain.com/work/the-quarry/)). Eyes carry the realism; never let them drift.
- **Performance.** Actors were directed, not demonstrated to; capture felt like stage acting ([MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/development-news/creating-the-atmosphere-of-until-dawn/); [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/the-quarry-used-the-facial-animation-tool-made-for-thanos)). Subtle acting enabled slow character change over the game.
- **Dread before scares.** Byles' lessons: foreshadow, give a clear-risk choice (the trapdoor you may or may not open), establish rules then break them ([SVG](https://www.svg.com/940858/director-will-byles-on-the-quarry-until-dawn-horror-film-inspirations-and-more-exclusive-interview/); [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/why-the-quarry-s-director-says-the-teen-horror-genre-is-great-for-games)). Death is permanent, "playing for keeps" ([Game Developer, Heaton GDC 2023](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/evolving-a-horror-aesthetic-with-the-dark-pictures-anthology)).
- **Fake-outs via player position.** Music stems swell as the player nears a door, so the same approach can foreshadow or fake a scare ([MCV, Pratt](https://mcvuk.com/?p=66454)).

## 4. Creature presentation

- **Rules the player learns.** Until Dawn's wendigos see only movement: a "Don't Move" prompt requires holding the controller still; if you move, you fail ([Critical Video Game Studies](https://criticalvideogamestudies.com/dont-move-an-examination-of-waiting-and-inaction-in-games/)). The remake kept it and added a "Stay Calm" alternative ([fandom](https://until-dawn.fandom.com/wiki/Until_Dawn_(2024_Remake))). Reviewers note players hold their breath anyway ([Gaming Bible](https://gamingbible.com/reviews/until-dawn-remake-review-697723-20241007)). Stillness under threat is one of the series' strongest tools.
- **Near-unkillable.** Shotguns only knock wendigos down long enough to flee; fire is the one thing that kills them ([Prima](https://primagames.com/news/until-dawn-chapter-8-3-hours-until-dawn); [Shamus Young](https://www.shamusyoung.com/twentysidedtale/?p=36650)). Fight to escape, never to win.
- **Heard before seen.** The Quarry's prologue ambiences seed distant howls; at the treehouse trapdoor, the creature is heard but never shown ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)). A cut to the creature's point of view (an *Evil Dead* nod) shows it stalking.
- **The monster is a person.** Each Quarry wolf was built on its human character; Byles wanted "something inside of you that's bursting to get out" ([SVG](https://www.svg.com/940858/director-will-byles-on-the-quarry-until-dawn-horror-film-inspirations-and-more-exclusive-interview/)). Wendigos are former people (the trapped miners). Give your creature human traces: proportions, a scrap of clothing, a human cry in the shriek.
- **Vocal change in the remake.** The remake's wendigos moved from long screeches to roars, growls and gurgles ([fandom](https://until-dawn.fandom.com/wiki/Until_Dawn_(2024_Remake))); fans debated it. Your owner's brief (torn human shriek) is closer to the 2015 original.

## 5. Sound

- **Ambience as a blend of 5-6 loops, reshaped per shot.** In Wwise, states keyed to shot type (close-up, wide, high, low) changed each loop's EQ, pitch, volume and effects; 3D emitters put insects on paths, leaf rustle in trees, and wind that changes with camera height ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)). In first person, drive the same parameters from interior/exterior, height and enclosure.
- **Ambience louder than film.** Pushed up for immersion, especially when the player is alone ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)).
- **Strip ambience to tension spots indoors.** In the lodge, ambience dropped to wind down a chimney flue and rain on windows, with more reverb on the voice ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)).
- **Silence needs a bed to cut.** Constance's lamp going out cuts all sound; before the lodge power cut, a subtle underscore was added so removing it would land ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)).
- **Creature vocals.** Many animal recordings layered, pitched down and EQ'd, with team members' own vocals matched to picture; a shared library because everyone tracklaid them ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)). Pratt turned a shower door screech with "a really special mid range component" into a creature scream ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=370143)). Unusual non-animal sources keep it from sounding stock.
- **Creature footsteps that cut through.** A processed stomach slap layered on the attack of each werewolf footstep ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)).
- **Jump-scare envelope.** Make the front of the sound "artificially loud", then drop the tail to a sensible level ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=370143)). Sweeten cinematic hits with pitched-down creature layers; use Shepard-tone risers because decision timing varies ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)).
- **Foley per body part.** 4,000+ sequences, per-character tracks for cloth, feet, pats and props, each on its own emitter (left foot, chest, hand); footsteps re-recorded when the ground went wet ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)). Until Dawn gave each character a unique parka "slip" or leather "creak", procedural triggers plus film-style layers on top ([MCV, Pratt](https://mcvuk.com/?p=66454)).
- **Music as stems.** Graves delivered up to 19 stems per cue; Ian Livingstone delivered 1-1.5 minute loops with 18+ stems, stingers and overlays ([MCV](https://mcvuk.com/?p=66454); [A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)). Graves scraped a piano soundboard with razor blades, bowed strings with pencils, and used a goat-hoof shaker to evoke the mountain ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2015/07/31/how-until-dawns-music-aims-to-get-under-your-skin/); [Nerdist](https://archive.nerdist.com/interview-until-dawn-composer-jason-graves-talks-horror-games-and-the-butterfly-effect)). Atonal SFX "can work with any kind of key" ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=370143)).
- **Mix priority.** Dialogue on top; high-impact sounds duck the music ([TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=370143)). 99.9% of speech runs through in-game reverbs ([A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/the-quarry-game-audio/)). Pratt's GDC talk covers panning rules that "place the player in the scene" ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026010/Adapting-Linear-Audio-Techniques-to)).

## 6. Prioritised checklist for Hollow Acre (UE 5.8)

1. Lock exposure (manual or tight min/max EV per volume) so Lumen and auto-exposure never lift night scenes to grey.
2. Use one dominant, low, cold moon directional light with crisp shadows, and set skylight intensity low enough that areas out of the moon read near-black.
3. Apply a blue-biased, high-contrast grade in the post-process volume (cool shadows, slightly warm highlights), and A/B it against Until Dawn 2015 frames, not the remake.
4. Light the camp with sparse warm practicals (lanterns, a stove, a fire barrel) as islands; leave the gaps between them dark.
5. Make the flashlight the only fill: a cold-white spot with shadows, volumetric scattering in fog and falling snow, and a dim falloff at the frame edges.
6. Use lighting channels and Sequencer-spawned per-shot lights in cutscenes, including rim/backlight on faces and hair.
7. Model each mine building as it was when it worked, then wreck it (collapsed roof, drifted snow inside, tools left mid-task) so the ruin has a believable history.
8. Lay a chain of 6-10 storytelling props (notes, photos, a dead radio, the journal) that foreshadow the creature and every scripted scare.
9. Keep the playable path a narrow trail, but frame huge Megascans ridgelines, the moon and the headframe as repeat landmarks from many angles.
10. Choose one real mountain range and match its conifers, rock and snow with Megascans, so the place reads as real.
11. Add functional tasks (restart a generator, flip a fuse box, light a lantern) that justify routes and turn light on and off on cue.
12. Build ambience as a MetaSound blend of 5-6 loops (wind, distant creaking timber, snow hiss, trees, far machinery) with parameters driven by height, interior/exterior and enclosure.
13. Place 3D emitters for wind through gaps, loose sheet metal, tree creaks and snow sliding off roofs, and keep the ambience mixed louder than a film would.
14. Before every big scare, raise a subtle underscore or hum, then cut all sound to silence just before the hit.
15. Shape jump-scare sounds with a loud attack and a quickly lowered tail, layer pitched-down creature vocals under cinematic hits, and use Shepard-tone risers for waits of uncertain length.
16. Build the creature shriek from layered human screams plus odd non-animal sources (metal screech, door squeal), pitched and EQ'd, and keep a shared, logged library.
17. Give the creature a distinct footstep transient (processed thump on the attack) so its steps cut through the wind.
18. Seed the creature by sound first (distant cries in early ambience, one heard-not-seen moment) before any clear sighting.
19. Add one learnable creature rule (it tracks movement or light; hold still or kill the torch) and make weapons only buy escape time, never kill.
20. Write music as stems with stingers and overlays driven by player distance to threats or doors, and precompile PSOs so cutscenes never stutter.
